Read the data carefully. ALA records are observations, not population counts. DCCEEW Biologically Important Areas identify places important for behaviours such as migration, reproduction, foraging or resting; they are not complete species ranges.
01

Recorded evidence

Where have marine animals been recorded?

The first maps deliberately answer different spatial questions: individual records show locations, while proportional symbols summarise where evidence is concentrated and where more focal species are represented.

Individual occurrence records around Australia

Each dot is one sampled ALA occurrence record. Use the species menu to isolate one animal and reduce overlap.

Why this idiom? Position is the most direct channel for showing where individual observations occurred. Dense clusters may reflect observer effort as well as animal presence.

Where is observation evidence concentrated?

Records are grouped into 2° cells. Circle size encodes record volume; colour encodes how many of the five focal species are represented.

New insight: a large circle means many records; a darker circle means broader focal-species representation. They are deliberately different channels.

How uneven is the downloaded evidence?

This checks dataset coverage before making ecological comparisons. It is not an abundance ranking.

02

Conservation risk

Risk is not one number — and pressures are shared.

The status ladder shows national EPBC listing level, then a network diagram reveals which documented pressures connect several focal species at once.

Which focal species are nationally threatened?

The grey nurse shark marker refers specifically to the east coast population.

Important nuance: the west coast grey nurse shark population is listed Vulnerable, not Critically Endangered.

CE
Grey nurse shark — eastern population

Critically Endangered; the current Action Plan profile gives an estimate of about 1,100 individuals.

EN
Australian sea lion

Endangered, with ongoing risks from fisheries interactions, debris and heat-related pup mortality.

VU
Green turtle

Vulnerable, Migratory and Marine under the EPBC Act.

Which pressures connect multiple species?

Each line links a focal species to a pressure explicitly documented in the selected Australian Government sources. Hover a species or pressure to isolate its connections; larger nodes have more documented links.

Why a network? The task is relational rather than numerical: it reveals shared pressures without implying that one threat is more severe simply because it has more links.

03

Places that matter

Why are particular marine areas biologically important?

Here geography and comparison are separated: the map shows where important-use areas occur, while the matrix makes species × behaviour differences easier to compare.

Biologically Important Areas by behaviour

Filter by species and behaviour. Colour represents why an area matters: migration, reproduction, foraging or resting.

Map meaning: a BIA is a biologically important use area, not a complete distribution range.

How does the mapped behaviour profile differ between species?

Cell intensity encodes the number of mapped BIA features for each species × behaviour combination.

Use with care: counts describe mapped BIA features, not the ecological size or importance of each behaviour.

04

Seasonality

When does the recorded evidence change through the year?

Two different advanced idioms answer complementary questions: a radial bar emphasises the repeating annual cycle for one species, while a bump chart compares relative monthly rank across species.

A year in one circle

Months are cyclic, so the radial layout reconnects December to January instead of treating the year as an open-ended line.

Which species ranks highest each month?

Use the focus menu to highlight one species. Rank reduces the dominance of species with very large raw record counts.

05

Human interaction

Catch volume and recorded mortality are different questions.

The Queensland Shark Control Program provides a direct human-interaction measure. The paired view separates the total number caught from the share recorded deceased at capture.

Program catch volume and the share recorded deceased

The upper bars show total animals caught each year; the lower line shows the proportion recorded deceased at capture.

Caution: this is not a trend in total marine-animal deaths or wild population size. The latest year may also be incomplete depending on the source refresh date.

06

Protection

Important habitat and legal protection are not the same layer.

The overlay map lets you visually compare one species and one BIA behaviour against Commonwealth marine parks, while the zoning chart explains how protection is managed across park networks.

Where do BIAs visually meet Commonwealth marine parks?

Teal polygons are Commonwealth marine parks; the orange dashed boundary is the selected species' BIA. Use the behaviour filter to ask a more specific spatial question.

Do not read this as “% protected”. A true percentage requires geospatial intersection and area calculation.

How does marine-park zoning mix differ between networks?

Each row sums to 100%, making differences in zoning composition directly comparable even when networks differ in total area.

07

Reading the whole story

Three ideas to carry forward

1

Observation evidence is uneven.
ALA records show where evidence is concentrated, not where the most animals necessarily live.

2

Pressures are shared, but conservation status differs.
The network shows common pressures across species while the EPBC ladder shows very different listing levels.

3

Important habitat and legal protection answer different questions.
BIA and marine-park layers are most informative when read together, not interchangeably.
