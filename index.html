How to read the data. ALA records are observations, not population counts. DCCEEW Biologically Important Areas identify places used for behaviours such as migration, reproduction, foraging or resting; they are not complete species ranges.

Where is observation evidence concentrated?

Records are grouped into 2° × 2° geographic cells. Circle size shows record volume; colour shows how many focal species are represented in each cell.

Larger circles mean more records. Darker circles mean more of the five focal species are represented.

How uneven is the downloaded evidence?

Conservation risk

Official status and documented pressures tell different stories.

EPBC listing gives a formal conservation category, while the relationship view shows which broad pressures are documented across several focal species.

Which focal species are nationally threatened?

The grey nurse shark marker refers specifically to the east coast population.

Important nuance: the west coast grey nurse shark population is listed Vulnerable, not Critically Endangered.

How to read EPBC status

These are ordered legal conservation categories, not numerical scores. A higher category means a higher assessed extinction risk.

CE
Critically Endangered

Highest threatened category shown here. The eastern grey nurse shark population is in this category.

EN
Endangered

Australian sea lion is nationally listed as Endangered.

VU
Vulnerable

Green turtle is listed as Vulnerable under the EPBC Act.

—
Not EPBC threatened

This does not mean “no risk”; it means the species is not nationally listed in one of the threatened categories shown above.

Population context: the DCCEEW Action Plan profile estimates roughly 1,100 individuals for the eastern grey nurse shark population.

Human interaction

Catch volume and recorded mortality are different questions.

Queensland Shark Control Program records provide a direct human-interaction measure: annual catch volume and the share recorded deceased at capture.

Program catch volume and the share recorded deceased

The upper bars show total animals caught each year; the lower line shows the proportion recorded deceased at capture.

This is not a trend in total marine-animal deaths or wild population size. The latest year may also be incomplete depending on the source refresh date.

Sources & method

How the visualisation was built

The story combines occurrence records, biologically important areas, marine-park data, conservation references and shark-control records.

Primary datasets

  • Atlas of Living Australia — focal-species occurrence records. Add the DOI generated for your exact download before submission.
  • DCCEEW Biologically Important Areas — migration, reproduction, foraging and resting polygons.
  • DCCEEW Australian Marine Parks — park networks, zoning and area.
  • Queensland Shark Control Program — catch records and status at capture.

Conservation references

  • Grey nurse shark (eastern) — DCCEEW
  • Green turtle — DCCEEW
  • Australian sea lion — DCCEEW
  • Whale and dolphin entanglements — DCCEEW

Cartographic method

Australia-wide maps use an equal-area conic projection. DCCEEW polygon layers are clipped to an Australia-wide viewing window, validated, simplified and rewound before rendering.

Reading the charts

Occurrence records represent observation evidence rather than population size. BIA polygons represent documented important-use areas rather than complete species ranges. Tooltips retain the underlying values where detail is useful.
